Six articulated residents move among twelve growing colonies in one physical world. Sight, recurrent neural activity, private memory, bodily cost, material exchange and a changing habitat meet on every tick.

This recorded resident carried 40 cumulative training updates. Its life in the reef is evidence to inspect, not competence assumed in advance.

One coupled world

The interesting part happens between the organs.

Chreatures is built around consequences that cross boundaries. A movement changes what the eyes can encounter. Activity draws on finite reserves. A colony changes collision geometry and chemical transport. Solar position changes illumination and phototrophic energy. Another body, a moved object, a sound, or a signal can enter the next private sensory state.

01

A measured neural scaffold

MaleCNS v1.0 supplies 165,122 traced brain-and-cord neurons, 25,563,197 directed edges, and 124,025,046 measured synapses represented by those edges. Native recurrent dynamics run against that anatomy; the dynamics and interfaces are engineered.

Anatomy and provenance ↗
02

Vision from the body

The physical head carries 1,024 collision rays: an 8 × 32 peripheral field and a 24 × 32 central field, each with RGB and proximity. Gaze and full body rotation move the lens. Bodies, branches, terrain and objects can occlude it.

Native sensorium ↗
03

A private remembered direction

Each resident keeps a private reservoir of experienced four-frame sensory encounters. A learned manager can select one as a current goal; the controller compares candidate movement with that remembered sensory and bodily state. A remembered state is not a map or a promise that the body can return.

Cognitive interfaces ↗
04

Consequences learned in the body

The current resident uses a rich-domain GAM law bank for movement, energy cost and fatigue response, then learns bounded private corrections from delivered actions and physical outcomes. Coverage varies by context: the recorded late phase has 3.75% candidate coverage, while its completed private-update count advances from 3,207 to 3,290.

Consequence laws ↗
05

An ecology with geometry

Colonies grow collision-bearing branches and surfaces from conserved synthetic material. Construction changes passage, contact, illumination and finite-grid chemical transport. Mobile mouths acquire material; digestion changes private reserves; deposits and colony packets return matter to the shared world.

Material commons ↗
06

An outside that can answer

A native solar cycle moves light through the habitat and funds phototrophic chemistry. Physical objects can be moved; finite resources can be offered; light, sound and gesture can be introduced. Residents receive those events through ordinary senses, without object labels, names or world coordinates.

Built, measured, unfinished

A mechanism can work before a creature knows what to do with it.

A rich physical history. Collection completed 196,608 transitions across six articulated bodies in four worlds, retaining raw 1,024-ray vision, full-circuit population readouts, delivered actions and subsequent physical outcomes.

A trained and installed resident. The courtyard artifact carries 40 cumulative training updates. Its H1 sensory predictor and rich-domain GAM consequence bank were installed in this life; the observatory exposes their recorded progress, disagreement, domain coverage and selected correction without presenting them as hidden intent.

A later physical failure. After the published 200.65–313.50 second segment, the world later paused at model time 458.9 seconds when chemical-field source 68 acquired an out-of-range position during an incomplete tick. The last complete checkpoint is preserved as 9c030d466a18fdb36e643b2f9069506b6a88843939a74d5b66b184668fa13ad9. The incomplete tick was not recovered or included in the recording.

Training continued separately. An hbox grandchild completed 160 updates and 491,520 transitions at 224.76 resident transitions per second. Those shared weights are now exported for a new birth; they were not installed in the recorded courtyard life.

A prior life retained. The first Living Reef resident reached 10,033 ticks and ended with depleted energy. Its saved world remains paused, its public v1 recording is archived in Git, and its 24-node native Weave biography preserves the evidence chain.

What remains open. The courtyard episode records real motion, memory selection, forecasts, GAM coverage and private updates. It does not establish learned feeding, sustained energy regulation, durable habits or reciprocal social behavior. Those capabilities still have to appear in longer lives and controlled comparisons.

All evidence and limitations  ↗
  1. Earlier reference

    Compact FlyWire world

    A 6,789-neuron female FlyWire v783 subset powered the earlier 2D engine and bootstrap behaviors. It remains an explicitly separate archived substrate.

  2. Physical ecology

    Growth became persistent terrain

    Three supplied colony lineages built more than 1,600 physical parts in 180 model seconds. Material transfer, digestion, topology-sensitive transport and deterministic continuation were exercised in controlled runs.

  3. Embodiment

    Brain, cord, body and chemistry joined

    Full MaleCNS dynamics reached articulated residents with physical feeding, energy expenditure, private recurrent state and continuous action.

  4. Living Reef

    The habitat became a sensory world

    Rich body-bound vision, memory, consequence learning, growing material ecology, acoustic mechanisms and a moving solar cycle now share one runtime. The next result to judge is what a resident learns to do there.
