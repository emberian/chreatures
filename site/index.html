01 / The starting point

A nervous system is a beginning.

Chreatures began with a question: could actual fly wiring become the ancestor of a new digital species? The mobile residents now run simplified dynamics on a measured MaleCNS brain-and-cord graph, connected to physical bodies and limited senses.

The ambition has grown toward ecologies: organisms that acquire habits, develop different bodies, build structures, exchange resources and leave environments that shape later lives. Creatures is one inspiration; the changing worlds of Children of Time are another.

Inheritance→Development→Experience→Changed habitat↻

The anatomy is a constraint on our models. Physiology, neural dynamics, body mechanics and interfaces include explicit engineering choices. We are building synthetic organisms; we have not recovered the scanned animal.

02 / Lives have consequences

What can a life change?

Its own future

Residents retain private neural state, contextual memory and learned motor parameters. An inherited capacity and a remembered encounter are different kinds of state.

Useful long-term foraging, manipulation and durable individuality are still being developed.

Someone else’s world

Native chemistry can fund real branches and surfaces. Built material can remain as dead scaffold, become shelter or obstruction, and be transferred into another organism’s digestion.

The material transfer has been demonstrated through an intervention. A self-sustaining food web has not.

03 / A shared substrate

Different ways of living.

We want to explore a space of ecologies: rooted and mobile forms, different sensory capacities, developmental programs and eventually different neural organizations. Shared chemistry and ancestry can connect them while each keeps its own history.

Organism-built terrain is especially interesting. A branch may be tomorrow’s bridge. A persistent skeleton may become a nursery. Construction could change which places connect, who can reach them, and how light and chemicals move.

Can inhabitants make a world that gives rise to new ways of inhabiting it?

04 / Instruments for curiosity

Affection, with an observatory.

People can move objects, offer resources and make light or sound signals in the local habitat. The residents encounter those events through their senses. Alongside that experience, we want instruments for asking why a particular habit or relationship developed.

GAM

Rust-backed modeling for nonlinear physiology, developmental histories and causal investigations. Native fits already run on developmental telemetry.

Universal Weave

Branching records for lives, experiments and competing explanations. Our native adapter preserves event identities and evidence ancestry.

The scientific archive is separate from an organism’s memory. A compelling story remains a hypothesis we can investigate.

05 / Come closer

A workshop with the doors open.

The project is being built with human direction and parallel coding agents. It runs on Apple Silicon and AMD GPUs, with Rust kernels, MuJoCo physics and Python research tooling. Working commits are frequent, and several mechanisms are changing quickly.

The full habitat runs locally or on your compute nodes. This public site contains descriptions and recordings; it does not run a live nervous system or connect to a private resident.
