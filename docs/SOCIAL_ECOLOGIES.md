# Social ecologies: a research notebook

## What would count

Eusociality is more specific than residents helping one another. The conventional
test combines cooperative care of brood, overlapping adult generations, and a
reproductive division of labour. Even this terminology is debated: Crespi and
Yanega proposed reserving eusociality for societies with behaviourally distinct
castes established before reproductive maturity. For Chreatures, the conservative
rule is to report the three observable criteria separately and describe caste
only if reproductive opportunity becomes persistently unequal, rather than infer
it from a cluster of behaviours ([Crespi & Yanega, “The definition of
eusociality,” *Behavioral Ecology* 6, 1995](https://doi.org/10.1093/beheco/6.1.109)).

The current system cannot pass that test. It has persistent residents, physical
contact, local vision, bilateral chemical sampling, three-tone sound, private
physiology and learned motor state. The environment has conservative metabolism,
renewable but finite producers, transported chemical fields, finite-energy
acoustic mechanisms, manipulable rigid bodies, and transactional growth that can
leave persistent structures. Developmental genomes can describe bounded related
variants, but the running ecology has no embodied reproduction transaction,
dependent brood, offspring-care behaviour, or inheritance of neural modules by
newborn residents. Stable differences in foraging, resting, signaling, or
building would therefore demonstrate task preference or group cooperation, not
eusociality.

## Design principle

Start with one body plan and one action space. Let task demand be a sensed local
condition and let differences arise through energy, gut load, fatigue,
development, inherited continuous parameters, and private experience. In a
*Temnothorax* manipulation, leaner workers preferentially met increased foraging
demand, whereas response to added brood depended on different factors; the
colony reallocated work without requiring immutable job labels ([Robinson,
Feinerman & Franks, “Flexible task allocation and the organization of work in
ants,” *Proceedings B* 276, 2009](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2009.1244)).
The engineering analogue is a response opportunity: an empty cache, hungry brood,
a strong trail, an unfinished structure, or a sounding surface that any capable
resident can encounter and act upon. No observation should expose “worker,”
“queen,” caregiver identity, colony totals, source IDs, task labels, or world
coordinates.

Physical and chemical traces can carry coordination through time. Experiments on
*Lasius niger* found that deposited chemical information and a body-size template
together reproduced aspects of pillar and roof construction ([Khuong et al.,
“Stigmergic construction and topochemical information shape ant nest
architecture,” *PNAS* 113, 2016](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1509829113)). That is evidence
for a mechanism worth testing, not a universal recipe. Tracking two
*Macrotermes* species instead found that aggregation at active excavation sites
explained early building better than deposition driven by a putative cement
pheromone ([Green et al., “Excavation and aggregation as organizing factors in
de novo construction by mound-building termites,” *Proceedings B* 284,
2017](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2016.2730)). Chreatures
should therefore make chemical marking, geometry, recent contact sound, and the
presence of active bodies separable experimental factors.

## Buildable mechanisms

**A shared nursery.** Add brood as ordinary finite-mass physical entities created
by an all-or-none reproductive transaction. A parent must transfer named chemical
pools and ATP; the offspring receives a recorded related genome and starts with
no privileged parent signal. Maturation should require a time-integral of local
temperature or light, tissue resources, and contact-delivered food. Any resident
can deliver material while touching the brood. Unfed brood loses viability or
develops slowly. Adults remain present after maturation, making generation
overlap measurable. Reproduction temporarily reduces the parent's tissue and
energy but does not assign permanent sterility or a care role. Reproduction,
care, maturation, and death each need exact mass and energy receipts.

**Local exchange and common stores.** Add a bounded contact action that transfers
a requested fraction of gut resources to another body or to a physical cache.
The receiver can accept only available capacity. Transfer uses the existing
metabolic transaction and reports donor cost, accepted material, and rejected
overflow. A resident senses contact, its own load change, local odor, and visible
cache fullness; it does not read another resident's energy. Shared stores should
be movable, shadeable, depletable, and able to spoil or leak scent, so hoarding,
delivery, theft, and redistribution remain physical possibilities.

**Signals with real propagation.** Retain anonymous three-tone emission,
occlusion, distance falloff, resonant objects, and diffusing secretions. Add at
most a secretion action with a metabolic cost and bounded gland reservoir.
Signals convey no predefined meaning. A nursery pressure plate may sound when
brood or food changes its load; a trail remains useful only at the receiver's
antennae and decays or advects. This permits recruitment or alarm-like regularities
to emerge while keeping sender identity and intended task outside cognition.

**Construction with conserved material.** Couple grip and contact to pickup and
deposition of finite substrate. A deposit can extend an existing local growth bud
only after a topology batch validates and metabolism pays the declared tissue or
mineral vector plus mechanical work. Accepted geometry changes collision,
occlusion, illumination, and chemical transport through the existing static-field
sync. Structure persists after its builder leaves. This supports chambers,
bridges, ventilation barriers, and nursery shelter without a “build nest” action
or authored waypoint.

## Causal experiments

Run a preregistered sequence rather than selecting a social story after watching
one world.

1. **Cooperation baseline.** Compare one resident, groups with interaction
   disabled, and groups with contact exchange enabled under equal per-capita
   resource input. Cross clustered renewable food with dispersed transient food.
   Measure survival time, ATP shortfall, food recovered, inequality of gut loads,
   and wasted or spoiled mass.
2. **Nursery dependence.** Once reproduction exists, cross brood that needs
   repeated delivery with brood that develops from its initial endowment. Give
   residents either abundant independent nesting space or a scarce sheltered
   microclimate. Hover-wasp field manipulations found that helpers adopted only a
   small fraction of supplied independent nests and that older brood increased
   adoption, warning that “nest shortage” alone is not a sufficient ecological
   explanation ([Field et al., “Ecological constraints on independent nesting
   in facultatively eusocial hover wasps,” *Proceedings B* 265,
   1998](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.1998.0386)).
3. **Coordination channels.** In matched seeds, disable secretion, randomize a
   trail's spatial field while preserving total chemical mass, change decay,
   mute acoustic radiation while retaining mechanical damping, or block direct
   contact while preserving density. These interventions separate information
   from energy and crowding.
4. **Construction alternatives.** Compare chemical-on/geometry-on,
   chemical-off/geometry-on, chemical-on/geometry-randomized, and active-body
   aggregation with no persistent mark. Score shelter quality, route length,
   ventilation, brood exposure, material moved, and later residents' probability
   of continuing or removing a deposit.
5. **Plasticity and lineage.** Rear the same genome under different nutrition,
   density, and nursery demand, then place adults in a common assay. Also compare
   related variant genomes within one rearing condition. In facultatively social
   *Megalopta genalis*, social and solitary forms coexist, and developmental
   expression plasticity strongly predicted social-expression plasticity
   ([Kapheim et al., “Developmental plasticity shapes social traits and selection
   in a facultatively eusocial bee,” *PNAS* 117,
   2020](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2000344117)). The simulation
   can test the analogous causal separation of developmental environment from
   inherited parameters without claiming insect molecular homology.

Every run should preserve full conservation ledgers and exact replay checkpoints.
Task allocation can be quantified as resident-by-task mutual information,
switching latency after demand perturbation, and robustness after removing the
most active resident. Cooperation requires a group benefit beyond summed solitary
controls. Reproductive skew requires viable alternative reproductive opportunity,
not merely different action frequencies. A eusociality result additionally
requires observed brood care and overlapping generations across replicated
lineages. Until all three exist, the accurate conclusion is narrower: local
mechanisms produced, or failed to produce, a particular form of cooperative
ecology.
